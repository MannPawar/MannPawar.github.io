Why players behave the way they do

What makes people quit a game, and who prefers which kinds of play?

The Silent Killers of Player Retention

Churn study of Steam’s top 50 games · briefed and reviewed by Kent Rollins (General Intuition, formerly Riot and Epic)

Reviews pulled through the Steam Web API and weighted by playtime to find where players quit. A local DeepSeek-R1 extracted 141 root causes across 47 games. Churn comes in walls: optimization debt before 15 hours, a veteran wall past 400 hours, and genre friction where working mechanics break the rules players expect.

Neurodiversity and Play

Survey reanalysis · 1,603 board gamers

A reanalysis of a 1,603-respondent board-gamer survey using PCA, latent profiles and equivalence tests, asking how neurodivergent and neurotypical players differ in what and where they play. The platform-preference model reaches a cross-validated AUC of 0.86.

Characters that behave believably

Can a thousand NPCs hold opinions that shift with the world around them, and say things that match?

NPC Opinion Dynamics

Agent-based model with a GraphRAG dialogue layer

An opinion model of 1,010 Skyrim NPCs (run it below), run 101,000 times and validated against an energy-matched null with Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests. A GraphRAG dialogue layer on a local LLM keeps what each character says consistent with their stance 93% of the time.
